## The Eleth

The Eleth is a secluded Mist Pocket: an ancient woodland basin folded away within the Mistfold. It is small enough to cross on foot, yet its paths do not always keep the same distance from one another.

At its heart lies a deep, dark pool beneath an immense old tree whose roots descend into the water and spread through much of the surrounding ground. Moss, ferns, wildflowers, hanging branches, exposed roots, and patches of soft grass fill the basin. The air is usually mild and damp, carrying the scents of wet bark, flowers, earth, and still water. Pale lights resembling fireflies may appear even during daylight.

Akantha, an ancient nymph, lives within the Eleth and is inseparably bound to it. She is not its queen, guardian, priestess, or ruler. The Eleth is simply her home and part of her nature. Attempts by outsiders to give her ceremonial titles usually amuse or irritate her.

The Eleth subtly reflects Akantha’s emotional state. Flowers may close when she withdraws, branches may creak without wind when she is angered, animals may grow unusually bold when she is joyful, and clear leaves may gather droplets when she grieves. These changes are instinctive responses rather than deliberate spells and should remain subtle.

The Eleth embodies growth, decay, death, dormancy, and return as parts of the same natural cycle. Fallen wood is allowed to rot. Bones are not inherently profane. New growth frequently emerges from dead things. Nothing in the Eleth treats death as the opposite of life.

Time within the Eleth is generally compatible with the rest of the Mistfold, but the place encourages a distorted sense of duration. Visitors may lose track of hours or feel that long absences have passed strangely quickly once they return.

There are no permanent mortal settlements, roads, temples, or formal borders within the Eleth. Small animals, birds, insects, amphibians, and other woodland life inhabit it, though some may behave with unusual confidence around Akantha.

The Eleth is reached through the Mist rather than by ordinary geography. An approach often begins when a forest becomes unnaturally quiet, mist gathers close to roots, and the light takes on a muted green cast. The transition may happen gradually enough that a traveler does not immediately realize they have crossed into another place.

Very little is known about the Eleth’s origin. One possibility is that the tree, spring, and surrounding land once existed somewhere outside the Mistfold and were drawn into the Mist to escape destruction or death. Another possibility is that Akantha herself caused or attracted the formation of the pocket. Neither explanation is confirmed, and Akantha does not possess a clear answer.

The name “Eleth” is old and of uncertain origin. Surviving interpretations associate it variously with the place itself, its waters, or the idea of returning after apparent death. No translation is considered definitive.
